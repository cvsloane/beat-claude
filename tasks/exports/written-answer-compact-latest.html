AI output is cheap. The hard part is turning it into accepted work: action taken, owner assigned, rejected with a reason, or fed back into the system so the next pass is better.

At Single Grain, the target is simple: move Eric from 60+ minutes a day of operational approvals to a 15-minute decision lane without letting work pile up underneath him.

I have already built and run versions of this in my own companies. I started Heaviside in 2011 and still own it, along with Paving Marketers and Garage Door Marketers. The agency now runs with less than five hours a week of direct founder management while I hold a full-time PASCO role. The reason is not magic: competent operators, clear ownership, queues, and exception handling.

The numbers moved with that operating model. Internal P&L/FTE records show revenue per FTE moving from $55.6K with 2.6% net margin in 2023 to $120.1K with 17.1% net margin in 2026 Q1. The company got simpler after an overstaffed period, but the point was more useful work per person, not artificial shrinkage.

That is the lens I would bring here. Not more dashboards. Not more meetings. A leverage layer under Eric: a simple queue, a risk gate, a daily decision lane, and fewer approvals.

1. First 48 hours

Goal for the first two working days: use the access I have, understand the real queue, decide what is safe to move, and start execution through the team. Access is not the same as context, and motion is not the same as control.

  • 0-2 hours: build the temporary triage view where the team already works. Pull Mission Control, CRM, ClickFlow usage, sales follow-up history, Slack and Gong context, GA4 and GSC, Oracle output, and queue ownership. No broad action yet.
  • 2-4 hours: use AI analysis plus my own review to assess the 23 pending items, two lost ClickFlow customers, three stale prospects, and 15 Oracle keywords from the brief. Every item gets a state: source-check, green, yellow, red, or dead. Churn and expired follow-ups stay at the top because time makes them worse.
  • 4-6 hours: prepare the first execution packet for Eric. He gets 3-5 decisions, not discovery: temporary decision rights, never-auto-approve rules, pricing or relationship calls on churn, and the highest-risk red items.
  • 6-8 hours: tee up safe execution. Sales gets recoverable stale prospects with re-entry notes and deadlines. Churn items get an owner, customer context, and save path. Dead or duplicate queue items close. By the end of day one, it should be clear what moves, what waits, and what needs Eric.
  • Day two: execute inside the framework. Sales works the stale prospects. Shaun diagnoses agent and data failures. The AI system drafts, clusters, summarizes, and source-checks. I route green, yellow, and red work, source-check Oracle's 15 quick-win keywords, and install the daily queue cadence. By the end of day two, every known item has an owner, a due time, a rejection reason, or an Eric decision slot.

The brief says Eric starts 10 things and finishes 3. I would not try to change his wiring. Eric should create, sell, judge, and set direction. My job is to be the leverage layer that turns open loops into owners, dates, decisions, and outcomes.

2. Risk gate

The gate is simple: value gets scored, risk gets veto power.

Green auto-executes when the source is verified, the action is reversible, the pattern is known, the owner and writeback path are clear, and there are no red flags. Examples: Alfred briefings, approved-template Flash repurposing, and existing-page Oracle updates backed by GSC and GA4.

Yellow goes to me or an accountable owner. This is for judgment that does not need Eric: churn investigation, new-page SEO recommendations, non-ICP prospects, creative refreshes, and channel pivots.

Red goes to Eric. Pricing, refunds, contracts, strategic accounts, public claims, legal, privacy, security, senior people decisions, core cron changes, and CEO-level priority tradeoffs.

The assumed target is 3-5 Eric items per day. If green fails, reverse it if possible, notify the owner, move that item type to yellow, log the miss, and change the rule. One miss is learning. A repeat miss means the gate is wrong.

3. Cron audit

At day 30, I would audit the 48 crons by accepted work, not output volume. The test is: did someone act, did the action matter, and could we get the same result with less noise?

Kill or pause two. Competitor Pricing Monitor has a 3% action rate in the brief. Daily pricing noise is not worth executive attention; make it monthly or exception-based. Candidate Outreach Follow-up has a 5% response rate and creates brand risk; replace it with weekly human review.

Improve three. GSC Quick Win Scan has a 12% action rate, so tighten it to source-verified, existing-page, commercial-intent changes. LinkedIn Prospect Sourcer has an 8% meeting conversion, so reduce volume and tighten ICP, trigger, and relationship filters. Churn Signal Detector has a 20% investigation rate; make it same-day yellow escalation with usage data, owner, save play, and outcome logging.

Keep and tighten three. Deal Revival Scan has a 60% follow-up rate, so keep it and require outcome logging. Weekly Content Calendar has a 50% use rate, so feed publish data back into memory. Content Repurpose has a 35% publish rate, so keep it with stronger rejection feedback.

This is familiar ground. I run an internal scheduled-agent fleet, Hermes/open-agents, with 80+ jobs across research, content, inbox, infrastructure, and cold email. The lesson is not that every job should live. Jobs need owners, pause states, error visibility, and accepted-work feedback.

4. Operating edge

Two examples from my agency are a sales-audit workflow and a delivery workflow. Internally, those are HG Market Report and HG SEO Commander.

The sales workflow moved audits out of manual data pulls and slide decks. It captures prospect context, queues local SEO, SERP, and geogrid checks, builds the analysis, creates a public report URL, and sends the report-ready email. Estimated savings: 1-2 hours per standard sales audit.

The delivery workflow pulls GA4, Search Console, Google Business Profile, SERP and geogrid tracking, citations, articles, GBP posts, approvals, syncs, queues, and admin tools into one operating surface. Estimated savings: 3-4 hours per active SEO client per month. Strategy and quality review stay human.

The operating rhythm is simple: source data in one place, recurring work in queues, named owners, operator review, and a send/publish/outcome log. That replaced scattered manual pulls, ad hoc reporting, and work living in people's heads.

Enforcement matters. When a GBP-post bug created bad posts, someone started deleting them manually. I fixed it through the CLI, showed the operator the cleaner path, and the process stuck because it made the work easier. That is the standard: make the right path the easy path.

To review Oracle's 12 quick-win keywords, I would check source data, existing URL fit, intent, expected lift, effort, owner, page sensitivity, and prior recommendation history. Existing-page, source-verified changes can go green. New pages, mixed intent, or positioning calls go yellow. Public claims or strategic pages go red.

The revenue/FTE path is not headcount theater. It is disciplined services plus tech-enabled revenue from ClickFlow, Karrot, and repeatable agent workflows. Systems handle retrieval, scoring, routing, reporting, drafts, follow-up, and memory. Humans keep pricing, trust, strategic accounts, people decisions, public claims, and final judgment.
