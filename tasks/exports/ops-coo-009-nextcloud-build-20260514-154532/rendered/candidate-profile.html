## MMPI-Style Self-Assessment Summary

Note: this is the "MMPI-style" item from the challenge brief, not a clinical MMPI-2/MMPI-3 instrument. For this application, I used LLM-guided self-assessment plus Kolbe / Enneagram / MBTI-style operating-profile work, then compared the practical operating implications against Eric's profile in the brief.

I am a founder first. My current shorthand is **INTP / `6w5`, Kolbe `9-4-3-2`**. The Enneagram result is not a clean landslide: Type `1` and Type `6` tied at `15`, with Type `3` at `12` and Type `5` at `11`. I call it `6w5` because the Type `6` pattern has a coherent Type `5` wing, while Type `1`'s usual wings, Type `9` and Type `2`, were basically absent. Practical read: trust/security/preparedness core, with a strong standards/responsibility/error-correction overlay.

Kolbe: Fact Finder `9`, Follow Thru `4`, Quick Start `3`, Implementor `2`. I naturally source-check, define terms, compare options, and preserve enough structure for the work to keep moving. I am not another high-Quick-Start idea generator.

That matters for this role because the brief describes Eric as ENTJ / `8w7`, Kolbe Quick Start `9`, Follow Thru `2`, and self-rated weak on follow-through, organization, and workflow consistency. I would not try to make him less like that. I would use his strengths and build the follow-through layer around him: clear owners, dates, decision logs, memory, and accepted-work tracking.

My strongest traits for this role are high agency, attention to detail, systems thinking, and tolerance for ambiguity. I am comfortable sitting inside a messy business problem, building the tool or operating layer needed to solve it, deploying it, and owning whether it works.

The leadership proof is that my agency does not require daily founder intervention. I have sustained and grown it while holding a full-time role at PASCO, where I own SEO/digital marketing and now ML/AI work. Today my existing businesses take less than `5` hours/week to manage because I hire competent people, give them ownership, and avoid micromanagement. It also matters for this role because I am not relying on founder heroics to keep my existing businesses alive; they are built around delegated ownership and operating leverage. My default is to hire people who can get the job done, give them a clear scorecard and cadence, and step in only where ownership, judgment, or performance actually requires it.

The closest operating analogy is forward-deployed engineer inside my own companies. I do not just specify systems; I build and run them against real sales, delivery, reporting, client communication, and fulfillment problems.

The main risk is overbuilding. I can create capability faster than a team can absorb it, or build things people do not need, want, or use. I compensate by fighting capability sprawl and forcing tools back to accepted work, owner action, adoption, and measured outcomes.

I work best with a founder who does not want to micromanage, hires competent people, expects them to get the job done, and steps in only where CEO judgment is truly needed. That is the operating fit I would aim for here: Eric keeps creating leverage; I make sure the company does not leak the follow-through.
