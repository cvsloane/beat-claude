# Lessons

- When summarizing live hiring research, separate confirmed job-post facts from inferred mappings and benchmark estimates. Do not present role-to-challenge mappings, compensation, remoteness, or conflict assessments as factual unless the live posting or repo explicitly supports them.
- Do not conflate x-accel with HeavisideOS. x-accel is a separate proven gate/scoring/content system. HeavisideOS is currently early-stage in practice, mainly SlackBot summaries of recent client communications and paid ads/SEO performance, with the broader company brain / operating system as the direction.
- When a submission packet is blocked only on user approval, stop adding extra guardrails or process. Keep the next step simple: approval, targeted edits, or final assembly.
- When asking for approval, explain what each approval item is for and whether it is a brief requirement, supporting evidence, or a local QA safeguard. Do not present internal checks or candidate self-assessments as mysterious "tests."
- For `ops-coo-009`, do not draft the operational-experience section from incidental project examples when the applicant's core proof is 10+ years running an agency. Treat operational experience and MMPI-style self-assessment as interview-driven sections, not default approval copy.
- For applicant interviews, ask exactly one question at a time, including one sub-question at a time. Do not batch prompts.
- When drafting Chris's applicant voice, avoid polished consultant language. Use direct first-person operator language, plain verbs, and specific systems; keep required source labels without making the paragraph sound generated.
- Do not imply HG Market Report and HG SEO Commander are the only tools Chris has built. Present them as two concrete examples from a broader pattern of building operating tools inside his own companies.
- In the self-assessment, anchor Chris as founder first. FDE is a useful operating-style analogy, but the core story is founder-led re-engineering of Heaviside, PVM, and GDM around an intelligence layer with software-driven and CLI-driven operations.
- Before finalizing `ops-coo-009`, assess every personal section and packet emphasis against the actual GM job description, not only the challenge brief. The JD prioritizes operational leverage, revenue per employee, CEO dependency reduction, financial discipline, leadership bench, accountability, and AI-enabled infrastructure.
- For Heaviside RPE history, do not use deposit cash flow as the primary revenue source when the user asks for P&L. Use QuickBooks/AF P&L-style income and labor accounts where possible, and explicitly document the method for converting payroll/labor spend into FTE.
- When using payroll/labor data for the GM application, do not stop at labor spend. Break labor into estimated FTEs, preferably by domestic vs foreign labor pools, so revenue-per-employee claims are tied to a defensible headcount model.
- For Heaviside profitability/net margin history, use the existing P&L net profit per quarter as the source of truth. Do not reconstruct profit from raw transaction lines unless explicitly asked to audit the P&L.
- Do not assume Agency Financials QuickBooks data access needs re-auth just because a report sync fails. First distinguish OAuth/refresh-token state, local encryption/decryption state, API report-sync coverage, and already-ingested P&L/report data.
- For the Heaviside RPE/profitability story, focus the narrative around 2024 because that aligns with AI/system integrations. Describe the FTE reduction as sensible simplification after overstaffing, not artificial shrinkage; support that by showing profitability and business health improved.
- When new role evidence changes the application strategy, mark older "final" or approval-default packet files as superseded. Do not leave a stale approve-default path looking submission-ready after the thesis has changed.

- For `ops-coo-009`, include Hermes/open-agents scheduler-fleet evidence when discussing cron operating experience. Phrase it as bounded internal proof of operating a monitored 80+ job fleet with pause/error visibility, not as proof that every cron is high-value or enterprise-scale.
