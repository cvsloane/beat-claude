# Ops COO 009 - MMPI-Style Self-Assessment Candidate

Draft status: non-clinical candidate self-assessment generated from observed working patterns in this project and related vault context. Chris must review, edit, and approve before use. This is not a diagnosis.

## Key Strengths For The Role

- High drive and high tolerance for ambiguity. I am comfortable taking a messy, under-specified system and turning it into a control surface with owners, gates, artifacts, and next actions.
- Systems thinking. I naturally look for the loop underneath the task: input, source of truth, owner, decision rule, action, outcome, and memory update.
- Strong source discipline. I do not trust dashboards, AI answers, or narrative confidence until the source path is clear.
- Builder/operator mix. I can use AI, scripts, docs, evals, and workflows directly rather than only managing people who do.
- Low need for perfect context before acting. I can triage under uncertainty and tighten the system as evidence improves.

## Likely Failure Modes

- I can overbuild the control layer when a simpler owner/SLA/checklist would solve the immediate problem.
- I can hold too many threads in parallel and create more surface area than the team can absorb.
- I can be too direct when a team needs more context or buy-in before a process change.
- I may default to source gathering when the organization needs a fast reversible decision.
- I can underestimate adoption friction if a system is logically correct but not yet easy for the team to use.

## Compensating Mechanisms

- Use a green/yellow/red routing model so only ambiguous or irreversible work gets heavy process.
- Keep the CEO review list small and explicit: 3-5 real decisions/day `[Observed from brief]`.
- Put every process inside a visible cadence with owner, SLA, and closeout rule.
- Treat councils as yellow-tier judgment, not a default meeting.
- Review process load weekly and kill workflows that create review debt.
- Require outcome logs and memory updates so the system improves instead of just adding documentation.

## CEO / Team Fit

I fit best with a high-velocity founder who produces ideas, relationships, and strategic direction faster than the organization can operationalize them. I am useful when the CEO wants leverage, not constant agreement. The working contract should be explicit: the CEO sets red lines and strategic priorities; I own the operating rhythm, routing system, and follow-through.

The risk is mutual frustration if decision rights are vague. A high-velocity CEO can experience process as drag; I can experience repeated exceptions as system breakage. The mitigation is to define what stays human, what can be delegated, and what gets escalated.

## Packet Disclosure Candidate

> My operating profile is high-drive, systems-first, and comfortable with ambiguity. The strength is that I turn messy work into gates, owners, source records, and repeatable loops. The failure mode is overbuilding or pushing process faster than a team can adopt it. I compensate by making routing lightweight: green items execute, yellow items get owner/council review, red items go to the CEO. I work best with a founder who wants strategic leverage and is willing to let the operating system absorb non-red decisions.
